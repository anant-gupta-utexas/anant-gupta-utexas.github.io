The climb

Don't get caught up in titles. The title matters less than the capabilities you build and the value you deliver. Here's the ladder — each rung a decision, each decision a lesson.

2016
THE PIVOT
Trained as a civil engineer. Chose to start over.
By graduation it was clear that path wasn't the future. So: bottom rung of a new ladder, on purpose.
Lesson: it's okay to change direction when your path doesn't match your future.
2016–19
L1 · ANALYST
Learned the craft in fast, chaotic digital-marketing analytics.
SQL, Python, visualization — and the discovery that I loved the technical, data-heavy work while craving more structure.
Lesson: stay connected to your network; the next door usually opens through people.
2020–22
L2 · DATA SCIENTIST
Healthcare analytics, plus an intense analytics master's at UT Austin.
Ran lots of proof-of-concept modeling and learned the hard truth: POCs teach, but only production creates value. Grad school reframed data science as problem framing + delivery, not just modeling.
Lesson: learn to bridge the gap from prototype to production.
2022–
L3 · ARCHITECT
A mentor reset the whole trajectory with one idea.
"ML without operations and engineering is just research with zero business value. Engineering is a one-time investment that compounds forever."
So I did the unglamorous work — retraining, deploying, refactoring legacy code — until engineering clicked. Then everything changed: I started thinking like an architect before starting any project.
Lesson: engineering skills are force multipliers.
Now
→ L4 · SPECIALIST
The LLM chapter — the frontier meets the measurement instinct.
Building generative-AI systems where the novelty feeds a curious brain and the work balances research with engineering. Going deep on agentic systems, evaluation, and orchestration of long-running, human-in-the-loop AI work.

What I build personally

"Theory without practice is research; practice without production is a hobby."

Small, instrumented systems for my own life and learning — one measurement layer, one orchestrator. Built because the problems are interesting and I want to stay sharp. Nobody assigned any of this.

plumbrepo ↗
A measurement spine for multi-agent systems.
Four tables, two entry points; everything else is a query. Measures what telemetry tools miss — human acceptance, routing quality, handoff quality, cost-per-resolved-task. A failing production run becomes a regression test in one call.
The whole design is an exercise in restraint.
Python · SQLite · Pydantic
atlasrepo ↗
A local-first agent orchestrator and task runtime.
First customer: a multi-stage dev workflow (research → spec → design → review → code) where every stage emits a measurement span — the pipeline measures itself end to end. Git worktrees as the code-gen boundary.
Built to be measured, not just to run.
Python · CLI · SQLite · git worktrees

Curated ingestion for my knowledge base.
Capture → extract → classify: turns a deliberately-chosen information diet into structured, usable knowledge. Instrumented with plumb from day one.
The engine behind curation over algorithmic reading.
